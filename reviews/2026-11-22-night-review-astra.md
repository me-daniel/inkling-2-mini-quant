## Reply (verbatim)

1. **BLOCKING — Correct the H0 record.** Amendment 2 says “Both H0 rules fire.” The first fails by a wide margin. Hybrid is about **0.00763 above UD**, against a reported two-SE threshold of 0.00113. Only the mirror rule fires. On S, replacing IQ4_NL down with UD’s native down lowers KL by 0.00118 on cand and 0.00099 on the UD background. The rest-of-file differences are 0.00782 and approximately 0.00763. That supports a dominant gate_up contribution and a smaller, consistent down contribution. **Check:** Publish both paired down contrasts and their roughly 0.00019 interaction with chunk-level intervals. Use the unrounded results.

2. **BLOCKING — You isolated the gate_up tensors. You haven’t isolated allocation.** The gate_up swap recovers 104% of the **cand-to-mirror** drop and about **92% of the cand-to-UD** drop. It transfers the type map, encoded values, and expert scales together. UD also spends 233,570,304 extra gate_up bytes. The proposed 1–5 and 9–11 swaps localize gains but retain the same confound. **Decisive run:** Splice **only v2a’s gate_up tensors** into cand, preserving everything else byte-for-byte. Compare cand, this splice, and gu-ud on identical chunks. The first contrast measures the map change under your encoder, subject to verifying the cand encoding recipe. The second measures the remaining donor effect at the UD map. Predefine how small that remainder must be to call it negligible. A separate equal-byte map is needed to claim superior *placement* of bits.

3. **MAJOR — “17 of 17” answers a narrower question than the report implies.** These are layer averages over the same systematic 16-expert sample. Seven layers lack a same-type comparison, including **all of 1–5**. Your diagonal imatrix metric misses activation correlations, gated-product sensitivity, router weights, and downstream propagation across three visits. A rarely selected expert and a dominant expert get equal votes. A shared BF16 conversion error could also favor your weights in this comparison. **Check:** Verify both source revisions and scale conventions. Replay identical reference activations through all 128 experts in the disputed layers, recording route-weighted error after the gated product by pass. For the end-to-end ranking, repeat a prespecified subset against source-model logits as well as Q8_0. Layers 20–23 validate neither the scale handling in 0–19 nor the reference conversion.

4. **BLOCKING — Fix the uncertainty calculation and freeze the T3 decision.** `math.hypot` discards covariance between models on the same chunks. “Within 2 SE” cannot establish “at least as good.” Likewise, a CI containing one cannot establish approximate additivity. **Check:** Compute per-chunk differences first and report paired intervals, with an article/block sensitivity check for adjacent WikiText chunks. Preserve the registered readings and label corrected analyses. S is adequate for screening. One approved, untouched T3 is adequate for a narrow WikiText replication if you freeze the artifact, comparator, metric, and success threshold before opening it. The documents alternate between **cand and mirror** as the release hurdle. Settle that now. T1/T2 remain descriptive, and a reasoning claim needs fresh reasoning data.

5. **BLOCKING — The most informative per-pass run is baseline-route replay.** My leading hypothesis is that small perturbations change top-8 membership near the eighth/ninth boundary. A change on an early visit can redirect later visits. A 7.7-fold reduction in KL also corresponds to only about a 2.8-fold reduction in perturbation norm under local quadratic scaling, and moving from two to three bits changes its direction. **Decisive run:** Repeat the existing 3-bit singles/all experiment while forcing the baseline expert IDs at every token, layer, and visit. Recompute the mixture weights over those IDs. Compare the residual with the existing free-routing result on the same chunks, reporting absolute Fisher residual and denominator alongside \(r\). A large collapse implicates route switching. Persistence directs attention to smooth nonlinear interactions, cancellation in the denominator, or numerical precision. Check a short raw-FP32-logit slice before interpreting a tiny-amplitude rung. Upstream’s saved log probabilities are clipped and quantized. [llama.cpp implementation](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/perplexity/perplexity.cpp)

6. **MAJOR — H1–H5 are useful controls with narrower interpretations.** H1 groups the same evaluations by layer instead of visit. Every affected layer still shares weights across visits. Its larger \(r\) shows that the partition matters. H4 permutes the **same three draws** and supplies no new seed replication. H3’s scalar KL ratio can depart from one even with exactly additive logit changes, through Fisher cross terms. **Check:** Bootstrap the *paired difference* between partitions, inspect the three pairwise Fisher inner products, and repeat tied versus independent with fresh seed triplets. H5 tests block dependence. The tiny-amplitude experiment listed in the concept is absent from `perpass3.sh`.

7. **BLOCKING — Verify the scored model and tokens at runtime.** `v2a.sh` can print success for an empty gate_up selection or a subset. Its down override covers all 24 layers, while mirror replaces only 0–19. `--verify` and a count of supported tensor types cannot establish that the loader uses each scale once, resolves each pass slot correctly, or dispatches the intended Metal operation. **Check:** Require the exact expected 24 keys on both sides and a full tensor/metadata diff, including down layers 20–23. Trace pass-to-tensor and backend assignments on a short prompt. Compare expert outputs with independently dequantized matmuls. Run an identity splice through the same slot path. Hash the token IDs embedded in each reference KLD file against the intended split. Upstream KL mode reads evaluation tokens from that file, so `-f S.txt` alone cannot catch a stale reference. Its printed chunk rows are cumulative, another trap for an extractor claiming chunk SEs. [llama.cpp KL reader and reporting loop](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/perplexity/perplexity.cpp)

8. **MAJOR — Resume logic can silently mix experiments.** A `Mean KLD` marker, existing GGUF, JSON, or TSV row can survive a change of model, reference, binary, or slot map. `kl_rows` parses human-readable output by field position, `chunks()` silently drops parse failures, and duplicate condition names overwrite earlier values. `DRY=1` can create the TSV header and its exit trap can remove another same-project process’s GPU lock. **Check:** Bind cached outputs to input hashes and arguments, require 16 finite chunk values with matching token IDs, and reject conflicting duplicates. Run an interrupted/resumed fixture and a simultaneous dry run. Use atomic lock acquisition with a unique owner. These findings come from the supplied scripts. The underlying splicer, residual implementation, and raw runs were unavailable for independent verification.

9. **MAJOR — Prioritize the kernel, then the per-pass paper, then a conditional v2a. Drop the mirror as a default release.** For the kernel, test the real UD file end to end against CPU NVFP4 on identical tokens, confirm dispatch, and benchmark prompt processing and decoding on an idle Mac. Passing backend-op cases establishes neither end-to-end parity nor the disappearance of a speed edge. Keep the mirror as a compatibility release only if it has a measured advantage on a supported build. An independently encoded v2a is legitimately **your quantization using Unsloth’s per-layer gate_up map**. Credit the exact UD artifact and revision, identify your BF16 source, calibration provenance and encoder, and reserve release for the frozen T3 outcome.
