# Research sweep 3 (GPT-6.2 Sol, effort high, Codex CLI with web search, isolated; 2026-11-22)

## Prompt

Task: find the current best post-training methods that beat diagonal-importance (imatrix-style) GGUF quantization at 2-3 bits for MoE models without full retraining, with emphasis on what generalises across domains: full-covariance (GPTQ/OBQ-style) code selection, scale refitting, AutoRound / SignRound, QTIP / trellis codes, AQLM / PV-tuning, GSQ, EfficientQAT, layer-wise or block-wise KL distillation of scales only, calibration-set composition and size studies (domain mixes, chat templates, reasoning traces). For each: source, what it changes in the encoding, compute cost, whether the result fits an existing llama.cpp IQ/K-quant format byte for byte, and evidence about calibration-domain overfitting.
Context (all public): Inkling 2 Mini is an open-weight looped Mixture-of-Experts language model (Thinking Machines, Nov 2026). Its 24-layer stack runs layers 0-5 once, the core layers 6-17 three times with the same weights (P = 3 passes; passes 2 and 3 read the previous pass through small loop-control tensors), then layers 18-23 once. Experts ship in NVFP4. We quantize it to GGUF for llama.cpp on Apple Metal at about 11.5 GB (2-3 bit expert codes such as IQ2_S and IQ3_XXS, block scales). Our measured findings so far: (a) quantization error in the looped core is not additive across passes; (b) shrinking an expert's output gain (attenuation) compounds over the 48 layer visits; (c) a code refinement that minimises output error under the full per-expert input covariance (Gram) closes most of the gap to the best published quant on in-domain text but transfers poorly to reasoning traces when the Gram comes from one domain. Existing Inkling quants (Unsloth, bartowski, ggml-org, others) treat it as a plain transformer: no per-pass handling.
Rules for your answer: only cite sources you actually opened in this session, with the URL; mark anything you could not open as unverified. No speculation presented as fact. Be concise: a numbered list, then a short ranked recommendation.

## Reply (verbatim)

**GSQ-RCO has the strongest published case for 2–3-bit MoE deployment in stock GGUF. Fixed-code scale distillation is the first experiment I’d run on Inkling**, given your measured attenuation across passes. I found no verified comparison showing that any method beats a matched 11.5 GB Inkling imatrix baseline across prose, reasoning, and tool use. The public ggml-org and bartowski builds already use Inkling’s chat template for calibration. A pass-specific quantization objective remains unverified. [ggml-org release](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/Inkling-2-Mini-GGUF), [bartowski release](https://huggingface.co/bartowski/thinkingmachines_Inkling-2-Mini-GGUF)

1. **Full-Gram code selection — GPTQ/OBQ, MACaQ-R.** GPTQ uses a damped Gram factorization to compensate for rounding errors as codes are selected. Its usual scalar checkpoint does **not** directly fit IQ/K blocks. MACaQ-R searches legal GGUF codewords and signs and **does** preserve the native layout. Its IQ3_XXS appendix improves imatrix-initialized WikiText-2 and Pile perplexity on two dense Qwen models, while IQ3_S gains are small or mixed. Eight sweeps took 5–8 seconds for one example down-projection, excluding Gram collection. The released native MoE comparison is Q4_K_XL, so a controlled 2–3-bit MoE domain-transfer result remains unverified. [OBQ](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.11580), [GPTQ](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.17323), [MACaQ appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.10649#A3), [MACaQ-R MoE release](https://huggingface.co/huawei-csl/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-MACaQ-R-GGUF)

2. **Scale refitting with frozen codes.** A local Gram-weighted fit can solve for the FP16 superblock scale and enumerate legal subscale values. This reuses the Gram, requires no backpropagation, and **fits byte for byte** when the fit respects the stored scale fields. MACaQ’s added scale sweep lowered calibration error but failed to improve perplexity over code-only refinement in its 4B ablation. JointQ alternates scale and integer updates at 2.6–15.7× GPTQ’s quantization time and reduces seed variance in one 3-bit study. Its published scalar output has no direct IQ/K packing, and seed robustness leaves domain transfer open. [GGML block layouts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/ggml/src/ggml-common.h), [MACaQ](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.10649), [JointQ](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20384v1)

3. **Fixed-code KL distillation of scales.** Marco-Nano’s 8B MoE release trained only IQ block scales against teacher logits for 24–50M tokens. It reports 41% lower held-out KL for IQ2_S and 22% for IQ3_S against public imatrix files with different tensor allocations. The output is **native GGUF**. GPU-hours and cross-domain transfer are unverified. ThinkBit independently found that scale-only end-to-end KL recovery lowered held-out KL from 0.641 to 0.282 on dense Qwen3-1.7B, with a 268M-token recovery budget. Its published scalar grid has no direct IQ/K packing. Both are end-to-end logit results. A controlled layer- or block-local scale-only KL result on a 2–3-bit MoE remains unverified. [Marco-Nano experiment](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/31320), [ThinkBit](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34886)

4. **AutoRound / SignRoundV2.** Signed-gradient block reconstruction learns rounding and scales. V2 adds gradient-informed scale initialization and sensitivity-based bit allocation. Reported Llama-2-70B cost is 2.2 GPU-hours for V1 and 2.5–6 for V2. AutoRound exposes **native Q2_K and Q3_K** schemes. The paper’s generic W2A16 checkpoints and IQ2_S/IQ3_XXS are separate cases. At 2 bits, task-informed calibration gave V2 +1.83 points over a larger generic-text control. Gains concentrated in calibration task families, and deliberately wrong answer labels retained roughly 90% of the gain. The 3-bit Mixtral extension covers one task. [AutoRound](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.662.pdf), [SignRoundV2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04746), [GGUF exporter](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/intel/auto-round/main/auto_round/export/export_to_gguf/config.py), [calibration controls](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11079v1)

5. **GSQ / GSQ-RCO.** Gumbel-softmax learns discrete assignments by block reconstruction. RCO selects tensor formats under a byte budget. **Native IQ2_S, IQ3_XXS, and K-quant outputs are demonstrated.** Dense Qwen3-8B Q2_K improved from 50.03 to 56.28 across three reasoning benchmarks after GSQ refinement. The Flash-Next MoE release reports 92.57 versus 93.12 BF16 on math, science, and coding at 3.00 transformer bpw. That release compares against BF16 and leaves the matched imatrix gain and calibration-domain effect unisolated. Compute is substantial. One published scalar recipe used 100M calibration tokens for ten epochs, and the RCO workflow also builds candidate tensor encodings. [GSQ](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18556), [RCO](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00649), [Flash-Next MoE release](https://huggingface.co/ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RCO-GGUF), [compute comparison](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28537v2)

6. **QTIP / YAQA trellis codes.** QTIP combines Hadamard incoherence processing, Hessian feedback, and Viterbi encoding. YAQA adds Fisher-weighted curvature tied to model-output KL. They **require a different decoder** from stock IQ/K. A 2026 comparison reports 15 PFLOP for base YAQA on Qwen3-30B-A3B, with optional codebook recovery adding a distillation pass. YAQA led the tested 2-bit methods on best generation for both MoEs, while changing the calibration mix moved Qwen3-MoE generation by 13.5 points versus 3.9 for QA. Trellis quality is still sensitive to calibration. [QTIP](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11235), [YAQA and MoE comparison](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28537v2)

7. **AQLM / PV-Tuning.** AQLM encodes weight groups as sums of learned vectors. PV-Tuning alternates updates to continuous parameters and discrete assignments. The published vector checkpoints **require a different decoder**. AQLM reports about a day on one A100 for 7B. PV-Tuning’s longest run took two days on eight GPUs to beat its baselines. AQLM saw diminishing perplexity returns from 128 to 4,096 calibration sequences. The papers establish low-bit quality, with cross-domain calibration transfer still unverified. [AQLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06118), [PV-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14852)

8. **EfficientQAT.** Block-AP updates all weights one block at a time, then E2E-QP freezes integer codes and tunes scales by default. Its published uniform groups **have no direct IQ/K packing**. Llama-2-70B took about 41 hours on one A100, including 14.3 hours for the scale stage. Dense ablations show a matched-domain perplexity advantage for WikiText calibration and better average zero-shot results with diverse C4 or RedPajama. Scale-stage average accuracy largely plateaued at 4,096 samples. On Qwen3-MoE, its best-QA checkpoint gave up substantial generation quality. [EfficientQAT](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11062), [2-bit MoE comparison](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28537v2)

9. **Calibration composition, size, and templates.** This changes the statistics or targets used by the encoder and **preserves the chosen format**. Cost is calibration forwards plus trace generation if needed. MoEQuant’s expert-balanced sampling and router-affinity weighting improved 3-bit GPTQ on DeepSeek-MoE and Mixtral. AYOT found that self-generated reasoning traces beat generic text, domain prompts, and stronger-model traces on its math/code evaluations. Mixing in generic text recovered language-modeling and commonsense performance. Its controlled size sweep favored 2M over 256K tokens at equal optimizer steps. Correct chat rendering has direct evidence for token coverage, while an isolated 2–3-bit Inkling task-quality gain from the template remains unverified. [MoEQuant](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03804), [AYOT](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01078), [GGUF template and size measurements](https://huggingface.co/datasets/AtomicChat/calib-corpora/blob/main/METHOD.md)

**Ranked recommendation**

1. **Run a matched-format pilot with two independent changes.** Compare frozen-code scale KL through all three passes with legal-code refinement using a mixed-domain Gram. Collect routing and Grams by expert, pass, and domain, then fit one shared encoding. For the scale pilot, update one physical core block at a time through the full loop so the loss sees the compounded gain.
2. **Use GSQ-RCO as the higher-compute native challenger at 11.5 GB.** Hold tensor allocation fixed for the encoder comparison, then let RCO allocate at the same total bytes. Use equal-token calibration sweeps and held-out prose, chat/tool, code, and reasoning generation. Choose by task performance across domains alongside KL.
3. **Use YAQA as a quality reference if a second runtime is acceptable.** It gives you a 2-bit MoE comparison that tests how much quality the native IQ/K representation may be leaving on the table.
