## Reply (verbatim)

1. [inference] **Parts 1 and 2 · Block layout and bytes.** A 1,152-wide row fits both 32- and 64-weight blocks. IQ4_NL uses 16 packed index bytes and one FP16 scale per 32 weights. Its fixed, nonuniform lookup table and 4.5 bpw are correctly described. GGML NVFP4 uses 32 packed E2M1 bytes and four unsigned E4M3 scale bytes per 64 weights, one scale per 16. The Inkling per-expert multiplier is separate from the block. Both payloads are 4.5 bpw, but adding 20 FP32 scale vectors costs 10,240 bytes before GGUF overhead. Q2_0 also uses 64-weight blocks. The 191 MB, 96 MB and 287 MB down-tensor figures check out. [GGML block definitions](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/ggml/src/ggml-common.h) · [Native GGUF](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/Inkling-2-Mini-GGUF#notes)

2. [inference] **Parts 1, 2 and 3 · What A measures.** Part 3 is right about the roughly 20 GB original. Expert weights in layers 0–19 are already NVFP4, and layers 20–23 use MXFP8. The 52 GB BF16 rebuild is an upcast. Repack the original down codes and scales as a native baseline and check decoded-weight equality against the donor. The donor result changes format and encoded values together. It validates that substitution, while an encoder improvement requires a comparison against native repacking. [Original checkpoint card](https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini) · [GGUF conversion notes](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/Inkling-2-Mini-GGUF#notes)

3. [measured] **Parts 1, 2 and 3 · KL and competitive claims.** Part 2’s KL direction is right. \(G\) exponentiates the arithmetic mean of the five domain means of chunk log-ratios. Part 1’s “geometric mean … of the mean log KL ratio” describes a different calculation. Resampling paired whole documents within domains is sound. \(G_{\mathrm{v2c}/\mathrm{UD}}=1.116\) means v2c has 11.6% higher drift, or UD has 10.4% lower drift. “Ranking per byte” lacks a defined metric. The 9 GB Unsloth comparison remains unresolved, as Part 3 says.

4. [inference] **Parts 1, 2 and 3 · Equivalence and task intervals.** The donor’s 5.2% KL reduction on S is supported. “Ties Unsloth” overstates the comparison. Its S interval of \([-0.00145,+0.00029]\) includes zero and extends beyond the roughly 0.001 KL margin implied by 5% of UD’s S baseline. Use “difference unresolved” and predefine the equivalence test, as Part 3 proposes. For the task battery, identify whether the printed endpoints are separate one-sided 95% bounds or a two-sided interval. The reported bounds leave the claimed advantage unresolved.

5. [inference] **Parts 1, 2 and 3 · Pass count.** The 36-of-48 count and the reported fixed-\(P=3\) results are sound. In the small-error approximation, \(r\approx3\) requires aligned perturbations of similar magnitude. \(r=1/3\) means joint KL equals the mean isolated-visit KL and can arise from cancellation. Near-additivity gives no threefold return per byte, and any particular expert can be skipped by routing. Part 3’s matched-reference test is sound. The supported variable-pass model is a separate checkpoint, so reproduce \(P=3\) there before comparing pass counts. [Transformers model documentation](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/en/model_doc/inkling2_loop)

As Part 3 notes, loop membership and depth are confounded.
