# Task: which parts of a competitor's quantized files are worth adopting?

## Model and rig
- Inkling 2 Mini: looped MoE, 24 layers. Layers 6-17 run three times per token (P = 3) on the same weights; layers 0-5 and 18-23 run once. Experts per layer: one fused ffn_gate_up_exps and one ffn_down_exps tensor. down rows are 1152 wide, so 256-block types (IQ1/IQ2/IQ3, IQ4_XS, K-quants) cannot be used for downs; only 32-block types (IQ4_NL, Q2_0, Q4_0, Q5_0, Q8_0).
- Target: Apple Metal through llama.cpp; every tensor must be in a type Metal can multiply.
- Metric: KL(Q8_0 || file) per token at P = 3 on held-out text. One KL run on ~49k tokens costs ~4 min of GPU. Building a variant by copying tensors between files is a byte copy (minutes). Our code-level refinement costs ~25-31 CPU-min per layer.

## Our pipeline (released file "v2c", 11.57 GB)
1. Placement: gate_up per layer exactly like Unsloth's UD-IQ3_XXS (column v2c below); all downs IQ4_NL; non-expert weights uniform Q6_K; token_embd Q4_K; output Q5_K.
2. Encoding: gate_up IQ2_S/IQ3_XXS codes refined per expert under the full input Gram from calibration text (objective tr(E H E^T), H = G + 0.1 diag G). Only grid indices and signs change; llama-quantize's fp16 block scales and 4-bit sub-block scales stay. A row output-gain floor guards each move.
3. We have our own Q2_0 encoder for downs ("Q2_0O").
4. An earlier allocator worked on 5 blocks (B1 = layers 0-5, B2 = 6-11, B3 = 12-17, B4 = 18-19, B5 = 20-23), with one gate_up and one down type per block, from a measured block map and an additive model. Measured on our selection set S (KL; bytes):

## Reply (verbatim)

1. **Ranked adoption priorities**

   **Inference.** I would test same-format scale refinement first, then exact-byte exchanges between individual tensors. Those can improve KL at zero net bytes. Among upgrades that consume bytes, my first candidates are the loop’s value projections, followed by selected expert matrices. For a downgrade, rank by **KL lost per byte freed**. For a funded exchange, measure the pair directly. The queued runs provide no observed gain yet.

   **Measured.** Your five-block allocator predicted sel-0 best. On S it was worst at 0.03145, while sel-2 won at 0.02931. The joint-pass KL ranged from 0.87 to 1.23 times the sum of isolated-pass KLs. Pass three contributed most. v2c already has per-layer gate placement inherited from UD. The opportunity is to *select* those placements against your objective and extend that freedom to downs and attention.

   **From-source.** GSQ jointly optimizes relaxed grid assignments and group scales against reconstruction loss. Its GGUF experiments project back into K-quant formats. RCO evaluates teacher-to-candidate KL on the assembled model, uses straight-through assignment gradients, enforces an expected cost during optimization, and uses dynamic programming for discrete feasibility. 3. **Independent implementation**

   **Inference.** Joint scale/code refinement is well posed as a finite, format-constrained optimization. Start from your released solution. For one row and block, let \(a\) be its decoded vector at unit block scale, zero elsewhere. Let \(r\) be the target row minus the reconstruction from all other blocks. With codes and subscales fixed, the continuous optimum is
   \[
   d^*=\frac{a^\top H r}{a^\top H a}.
   \]
   Skip zero curvature. Score the neighboring legal FP16 values and the incumbent using the full-row objective. For each sub-block, enumerate its 16 legal nibble values with the actual decoder. Alternate these steps with your grid/sign moves. Keep strict improvements that pass the row-gain floor. Cache the Gram-times-residual vector and update it after accepted moves. Parallelize independent rows or experts.

   **From-source.** IQ2_S and IQ3_XXS encode subscales differently. IQ3_XXS packs its scale with sign information. [GGML decoders](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/ggml/src/ggml-quants.c)

   **Inference.** Pool routed calibration inputs from all three visits with their observed frequencies. Preserve c1’s gain correction in matched controls. Pilot one IQ2_S and one IQ3_XXS layer before expanding. For allocation, cache your own packed candidates and search byte-balanced exchanges with measured P=3 KL. This implements the joint-loss idea on your rig without requiring a differentiable Metal backend.

5. **What to leave behind**

   **Inference.** Skip wholesale tensor copying, blanket early starvation, automatic BF16 embedding promotion and a fixed threefold loop sensitivity rule. A same-type swap tests the entire encoding, including initialization and calibration. A win cannot identify scale learning alone. Keep 256-block types off 1152-wide downs and require a working Metal kernel for each candidate. Treat ISTA’s benchmark recovery as evidence for its stated tasks and teacher. Use your paired Q8_0 KL to decide the release.
