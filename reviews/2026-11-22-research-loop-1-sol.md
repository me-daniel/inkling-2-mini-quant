# Research sweep 1 (GPT-6.2 Sol, effort high, Codex CLI with web search, isolated; 2026-11-22)

## Prompt

Task: find published quantization, compression or calibration techniques designed for looped, recurrent-depth or weight-shared transformers (examples to check, not limits: Universal Transformers, ALBERT-style sharing, Relaxed Recursive Transformers with per-loop LoRA, Huginn / recurrent-depth latent reasoning, MoEUT, Ouro / LoopLM, Mixture-of-Recursions, any NVIDIA looped or recurrent model, possibly called "Dory"; verify whether a "Dory" from NVIDIA exists and what it is). For each technique: what it does, the reported gain, whether it needs training, and whether it could apply to a post-training GGUF quant of Inkling 2 Mini in llama.cpp (per-pass scales, per-loop adapters, loop-aware calibration, per-iteration precision, etc.). Flag any that nobody has applied to Inkling yet.
Context (all public): Inkling 2 Mini is an open-weight looped Mixture-of-Experts language model (Thinking Machines, Nov 2026). Its 24-layer stack runs layers 0-5 once, the core layers 6-17 three times with the same weights (P = 3 passes; passes 2 and 3 read the previous pass through small loop-control tensors), then layers 18-23 once. Experts ship in NVFP4. We quantize it to GGUF for llama.cpp on Apple Metal at about 11.5 GB (2-3 bit expert codes such as IQ2_S and IQ3_XXS, block scales). Our measured findings so far: (a) quantization error in the looped core is not additive across passes; (b) shrinking an expert's output gain (attenuation) compounds over the 48 layer visits; (c) a code refinement that minimises output error under the full per-expert input covariance (Gram) closes most of the gap to the best published quant on in-domain text but transfers poorly to reasoning traces when the Gram comes from one domain. Existing Inkling quants (Unsloth, bartowski, ggml-org, others) treat it as a plain transformer: no per-pass handling.
Rules for your answer: only cite sources you actually opened in this session, with the URL; mark anything you could not open as unverified. No speculation presented as fact. Be concise: a numbered list, then a short ranked recommendation.

## Reply (verbatim)

1. **Inkling already has a loop-aware GGUF precedent.** The public `mixB` release spends IQ3_XXS on the shared core and mostly IQ2_S elsewhere. It uses one weight copy across all three passes and requires a fork for padded expert rows. The author reports **10.3 GiB**, 90.9% top-token agreement and 1.034× perplexity versus Q8_0. That establishes loop-aware **layer allocation**. I found no public fixed-depth Inkling result demonstrating pass-indexed scales, pass-specific adapters, or full-Gram calibration stratified by pass and domain. [mixB model card](https://huggingface.co/hridya423/Inkling-2-Mini-12GB-GGUF), [fork and recipe](https://github.com/hridaya423/llama.cpp/blob/inkling-fast/docs/inkling2-fast.md)

2. **Trajectory GPTQ and Hyperloop are the closest weight-only precedents.** Both pool input Grams across visits to each shared tensor. On Huginn-3.5B, trajectory GPTQ raised GSM8K from **26.9% to 35.0%** at grouped INT4 versus first-pass-only GPTQ, with BF16 at 34.9%. At INT3 it raised **5.84% to 20.70%**. The same study identified Huginn’s loop-entry adapter as particularly sensitive. Hyperloop used all-pass GPTQ on a three-pass middle core and reported **8.59 INT4 perplexity versus 8.49 BF16** on its largest model. The GPTQ calibration needs no training. For Inkling, pool routed per-expert Grams from all three passes and ablate the message-control tensors separately. The resulting IQ codes can keep the existing runtime layout. [Trajectory GPTQ](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.30820v1), [Hyperloop §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21254)

3. **Loopy selects one shared quant at deployment depth.** It searches channel scalings and rotations with forward-only calibration, advancing promising candidates to more calibration windows while always executing the full loop count. On Ouro-1.4B, W4A4 LAMBADA perplexity was **36.5% lower** than adapted SpinQuant. No training. The directly portable part is candidate selection. Rank shared IQ codes, block scales, or bit allocations by final prediction loss after all three Inkling passes. [Loopy](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05265v1)

4. **LoopQuant is the strongest precedent for pass-specific gain and precision.** It learns a shared transformation, searches activation bit widths jointly across passes, and fits per-pass group clipping, gain and bias with frozen weights. Ouro-2.6B W4A4 beat the paper’s W4A8 baselines, used **65.3% less model memory** and decoded **1.7× faster** than BF16 on NVIDIA hardware. It uses gradient-based calibration. For Inkling, a pass-indexed expert-output gain is a small first adaptation. Pass-specific activation precision needs graph and Metal support. Pass-specific **weight** precision would need another weight representation or a progressive encoding. [LoopQuant](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.16368v1)

5. **LoopQ explicitly calibrates loop transitions.** It fits pass-dependent activation scales, selectively uses pass-specific transforms, and learns transition adapters against both boundary states and final logits. Its revised paper reports **67.8% relative mean accuracy improvement** and **75.2% average relative perplexity reduction** versus the strongest static W4A4 baselines across four backbones. Calibration backpropagates through the loops with the backbone frozen. For Inkling, the analogue is fitting a small correction on the gated message path into passes 2 and 3. The full recipe needs runtime changes, and selected transforms require pass-dependent weight representations. [LoopQ v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16343v2), [Inkling loop implementation](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-11-20-inkling2-mini)

6. **NVIDIA Dory exists and ships a distilled GGUF.** Nemotron Labs Dory1.0-4B is a normalized GPT with a shared recurrent block that can run up to four passes. Weight-only MXFP8 PTQ was broadly comparable to BF16. NVIDIA used quantization-aware distillation for NVFP4 and **Q5_K_M GGUF**, matching teacher and student loop counts and updating all student weights. At four passes, Q5_K_M scored **72.66 versus 72.16** BF16 on GPQA Diamond and **18.26 versus 22.2** on Terminal-Bench. On DGX Spark, MXFP8 and NVFP4 reached **1.84× and 2.44×** BF16 speed. The distillation recipe could target Inkling’s IQ format with a training implementation. Its final-logit KL also works as a forward-only selection metric. [Dory report](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03998v1), [NVIDIA GGUF](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-Labs-Dory1.0-4B-GGUF)

7. **Relaxed Recursive Transformers provide the per-pass LoRA precedent.** They add depth-specific low-rank deltas to shared weights, initialize from an untied model with SVD, then uptrain and distill. Rank-512 relaxed Gemma reached **58.4% versus 58.6%** few-shot accuracy for full Gemma. ModernALBERT’s related Mixture of LoRAs trains routed adapters and merges them at inference, with MNLI **88.2 → 87.9** after merging. For Inkling, a pass-indexed low-rank correction could retain one IQ backbone, with adapter fitting and pass-aware dispatch. [Relaxed Recursive Transformers](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.20672v3), [ModernALBERT](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12880v1)

8. **Universal Transformer, ALBERT, MoEUT, Ouro and Mixture-of-Recursions establish architectural savings.** UT introduced depth reuse and adaptive halting. ALBERT-base used **12M versus 108M** parameters with sharing and factorized embeddings. MoEUT’s 1.04B configuration improved C4 perplexity **11.15 → 10.90**. Ouro trained shared-depth models with learned exit allocation. MoR trained token-level depth routers and reported up to **2.06×** throughput versus its vanilla baseline. These gains come from trained architectures. Inkling already shares its core, and adopting learned exits or routing would change the deployed model. [UT](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03819), [ALBERT](https://arxiv.org/html/1909.11942), [MoEUT](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16039), [Ouro](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25741), [MoR](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10524)

9. **Loop-aware KV compression can reduce long-context RAM.** ResidualQuant uses a final-pass INT4 anchor and INT2 residuals, with fitted rotations and **80.7% less theoretical KV storage**. LoopVQ fits joint cross-pass codebooks and beats its KV baselines at **0.5 bits per element**. LoopKV uses offline sensitivity profiling without model training and reports **35% less KV** than uniform 4-bit KIVI. NVIDIA’s last-loop overwrite needs no training and keeps attention KV at one-loop size. Thinking Machines’ **variable-loop research sibling** already exposes cache sharing in vLLM. The fixed three-pass release uses separate caches. These techniques target runtime memory, with cache and attention changes for llama.cpp/Metal. [ResidualQuant](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10381v1), [LoopVQ](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.05304v1), [LoopKV](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.04137v1), [NVIDIA last-loop KV](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12582), [vLLM Inkling support](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-11-20-inkling2-mini)

**Ranked recommendation**

1. **Start with pass- and domain-stratified Grams.** On the native NVFP4/MXFP8 reference, collect routed inputs by expert, pass and domain. At the same byte budget, compare first-pass, raw pooled and domain-balanced pooled Grams with equal calibration-row counts. Keep held-out reasoning traces separate from fitting. [Inkling release precision](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
2. **Select candidates after all three passes.** Use the Grams to generate shared IQ code and scale candidates. Rank them by final-logit KL and check held-out reasoning accuracy alongside text perplexity. Include a `mixB`-style allocation baseline and verify the actual expert tensor types, since incompatible row widths can cause IQ2/IQ3 fallback. [Loopy](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05265v1), [mixB padding details](https://github.com/hridaya423/llama.cpp/blob/inkling-fast/docs/inkling2-fast.md)
3. **If attenuation remains, hold codes fixed and fit near-unity gains.** Compare a shared gain with pass-indexed gains on selected expert outputs. Score both through the full recurrence. This isolates the effect of gain correction before adding per-pass adapters or precision changes. [LoopQuant gain ablation](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.16368v1)
